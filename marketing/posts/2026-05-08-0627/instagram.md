# You can't transform what you can't name.

You can't transform what you can't name.

There's a real gap between "I feel bad" and "I feel that specific hollow-dropping sensation after a conversation ends and you're not sure if it was something you said."

Lisa Feldman Barrett's emotional granularity research shows people with richer emotional vocabulary regulate their emotions more effectively — not because words are magic, but because precision changes what you can actually do next.

Matt Lieberman's fMRI work found that labeling an emotion precisely reduces amygdala activity ~40%. The brain responds differently to a name than to a fog.

Cathier's awareness dictionary has 79 emotion words across 9 categories. Three examples:

空落感 (kōng luò gǎn) — the sensation of dropping from somewhere you can't identify. Not emptiness. A fall.

婆娑 (pó suō) — not just eyes crying. The whole self in slow dissolution.

局促 (jú cù) — not shyness. Being bodily contained by the moment itself.

Each word is a finer instrument. The coarser your vocabulary, the coarser your moves.

Which word has been sitting with you today?
