# The word you're missing might be the one that helps.

Have you ever tried to describe how you're feeling and found that "sad" or "stressed" just doesn't quite reach it?

That gap has a name in psychology — emotional granularity. Research by Lisa Feldman Barrett found that people who can identify their emotions precisely tend to handle difficult situations more effectively. A separate brain-imaging study found that naming an emotion accurately — not vaguely — can reduce the stress response in the brain by nearly 40%.

It's not about having the "right" words. It's about having enough of them.

Cathier's awareness dictionary was built around this idea. It has 79 distinct emotion words across 9 categories. Not as a test, and not to add complexity — but because sometimes the word you're looking for is the difference between spinning and starting to move.

One example: 空落感 (kōng luò gǎn). It describes the sensation of dropping from somewhere you can't quite locate — not emptiness exactly, but a falling. Some people recognize it immediately. Some have never had a word for it before.

What word have you been missing?
