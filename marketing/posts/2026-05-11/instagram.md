# Suppressed anger doesn't vanish — it becomes resentment. Witnessed anger becomes awareness.

Suppressed anger doesn't vanish — it becomes resentment. Witnessed anger becomes awareness.

This is what Osho kept returning to: the gap between suppression and witnessing is the entire practice.

Most of us were taught to manage anger — count to ten, breathe through it. But you can't witness what you can't locate.

Cathier's body scan lets you name exactly where anger lives in this moment:

Chest tightness — often hurt wearing anger's face.
Throat constriction — something not yet said.
Hands tensing, wanting to move — a boundary that's been crossed.

These aren't the same anger. They're asking for different things.

Cathier holds 8 words for anger in the awareness dictionary: 愤慨 (righteous indignation), 不满 (dissatisfaction), 烦躁 (agitation), 暴怒 (rage), 憎恨 (contempt), 嫉妒 (envy), 厌恶 (disgust), 气愤 (anger). Naming which one — and where in your body — is the first act of witnessing.

Not managing. Witnessing.

That's the whole move.
