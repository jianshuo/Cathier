# Shame has a posture — and your spine knows it before you do.

Shame has a posture — and your spine knows it before you do.

Back curved. Shoulders drop. Eyes go down. Your body runs the whole script before you've finished saying "I'm fine."

Shame doesn't start in the head. It starts in the spine.

We built Cathier's awareness dictionary around this: eight words specifically for shame — not to process it away, but to let you recognize it precisely.

*愧怍 (kuì zuò)* — the sinking weight between your chest and navel after you've done something you regret. Not guilt exactly. Something that sits one floor lower.

*局促 (jú cù)* — the stiffness when you don't know where to put your hands. The body quietly looking for somewhere to hide.

*悚然 (sǒng rán)* — that single second when someone sees through you.

*惴惴 (zhuì zhuì)* — the body bracing for blame before anything has even started.

When you can say "this is 愧怍, not just feeling bad" — something in the spine begins to release.

Not to accept shame. Just to recognize it first. That's the whole practice.
