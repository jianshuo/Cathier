# Sadness doesn't need a solution. It needs to be seen.

Sadness doesn't need a solution. It needs to be seen.

If you've ever had that feeling — a heaviness in your chest, eyes that sting before you've even cried, a kind of dullness you can't quite put into words — you know what it's like to carry sadness without fully understanding it.

Most of us were taught to push through it. Or to talk about it in very broad terms: "I'm sad." "I'm not doing well." But "sad" is a large, vague container. Inside it are many different experiences that each need different kinds of attention.

Cathier has an awareness dictionary with 12 different words for sadness. Not to make things complicated — but to give the feeling a specific place to land. There's a word for the sadness of losing something you can't quite name. A word for when grief sits in your body more than your thoughts. A word for the quiet, low-grade heaviness that's been there so long you've stopped noticing it.

Naming it doesn't fix it. But it does something important: it lets you actually be with it, instead of constantly trying to get past it.

That's what Cathier's daily check-in is for — not to solve what you feel, but to help you see it clearly, one day at a time.
