# Body first

Most emotion apps ask "how are you feeling?" — Cathier asks you to check your body first.

Emotions don't begin in the mind. They begin somewhere in the body — the tightness in your throat before you can name what you're nervous about. The weight settling into your chest before any thought arrives to explain it.

Cathier's first step is a body scan. You choose a body part — throat, chest, stomach, shoulders, lower back, even your jaw. 24 locations in total. Then you describe what you feel there, not from a generic scale, but from a vocabulary of 65 physical sensations: "constricted," "hollow," "pulsing," "burning," "floating."

Once your body has said something, you name the emotion. Not the other way around.

This sequence mirrors how emotions actually work — bottom-up, body first. Practicing it daily builds what researchers call interoceptive awareness: your ability to read your body's internal signals before they become problems you can't name.

The body scan takes about 30 seconds. What it teaches takes longer.
