# Most people can't accurately feel their own heartbeat. That gap is where emotional blindness begins.

Most people can't accurately feel their own heartbeat. That gap is where emotional blindness begins.

Psychologists call this interoception — your ability to detect internal body signals: heart rate, breath rhythm, gut tension, the tightening in your chest before you know why.

Research by Khalsa and colleagues found that daily body scanning, practiced for 8 weeks, leads to a 38% improvement in interoceptive accuracy. The finding that matters more: interoceptive accuracy directly predicts emotional awareness. If you can't sense your body, you can't name what you feel. And feelings you can't name tend to stay stuck.

Cathier is built around this. Every check-in starts with a body scan — 24 specific areas, 65 somatic sensations. Not "how are you feeling today?" but something more specific: what is your throat doing right now? What is your gut saying? Where are your shoulders carrying something?

One scan is a moment of noticing. Thirty scans is a practice. A hundred scans is a measurably more body-literate version of you.

This is not a wellness habit. It's a trainable neurological skill.
