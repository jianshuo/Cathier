# The science behind knowing what you feel

Most people walk through the day without noticing what their body is telling them — the tightness in the chest before a hard conversation, the drop in the gut when something feels wrong, the breath that catches when stress arrives before the thought does.

Psychologists have a word for the ability to read these internal signals: interoception. Research by Khalsa and colleagues found that practicing a daily body scan for just 8 weeks led to a 38% improvement in this skill. And here's why that matters: better interoception is directly linked to better emotional awareness. When we lose touch with our body's signals, we lose the ability to name what we're feeling. Feelings we can't name tend to get stuck.

Cathier starts every check-in with a body scan across 24 specific areas — throat, chest, gut, shoulders, hands. Not "how are you feeling today?" but something more precise: what is your body actually saying right now?

Over time, those daily moments add up. It's a genuine skill, measurable and trainable, not just a wellness habit.
