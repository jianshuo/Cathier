# The one second before you react

Something happens. Someone says something. A message arrives. Traffic backs up. And before you've thought about it, your shoulders have already risen.

In that moment — between what landed on you and what you did with it — there's a gap.

Osho talked about it often. Viktor Frankl called it "the space where freedom lives." Somatic therapists call it the moment before the nervous system locks in a response.

Most emotion apps jump straight past it: "How do you feel?" They skip the gap entirely.

Cathier's check-in starts differently. There's a simple optional field — "What happened?" — just a few words about the event, before you name an emotion or scan your body.

That small pause is the whole practice.

Over time, those short notes start to reveal patterns. The situations that reliably produce tightness in your chest. The kinds of conversations that leave your jaw clenched. You're not just recording moods — you're learning to read your own triggers.

The gap is small. But it's where awareness begins.
