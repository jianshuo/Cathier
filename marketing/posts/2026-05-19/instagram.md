# Between what happened and what you felt, there's a gap.

Most emotion apps collapse that space instantly: "How are you feeling today?" — as if feelings arrive from nowhere.

Cathier's check-in includes a quiet optional field at the very start: "What happened?"

It's not required. But it's there — before you pick a body part, before you name an emotion.

Osho called this the gap: the one second between something landing on you and your reaction forming. Viktor Frankl called it the only space where freedom lives. In somatic practice, it's where the body starts responding before the mind catches up.

Most of us sprint through that second without knowing it existed.

The "What happened?" field asks you to pause there. To name the event before you name the feeling. To see what moved you before you decide what it means.

After 30 check-ins, patterns emerge. Certain situations keep appearing before certain body sensations. That's not coincidence — that's information.

The gap is where observation begins.
