If your shoulders feel heavy tonight, here's something worth sitting with: that weight often isn't from today. It's from tomorrow.

The tight, familiar ache in your upper back isn't always fatigue from what you've already done. More often, it's anticipation — the task you haven't started, the conversation you've been postponing, the obligation that isn't here yet but your body is already bracing for.

Nervous systems don't wait for problems to arrive before responding. They scan ahead and start preparing.

Cathier is a body-and-emotion awareness app with 24 body-part entries in its awareness dictionary. The shoulder section describes two distinct sensations worth knowing:

One for the weight of duty settling into muscle — not tiredness from effort, but obligation finding a physical home before the work even begins.

One for pre-tension: that "I'm not ready, but I have to be" tightening that arrives before the event does.

They feel similar. But they're asking very different questions.

Next time your shoulders feel heavy, try this: ask whether it's today's weight or tomorrow's making them that way. That single question often shifts what you do next.
