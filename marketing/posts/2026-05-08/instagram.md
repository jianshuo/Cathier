Your shoulders aren't carrying yesterday. They're already bracing for tomorrow.

That tight, familiar weight in your upper back — it's rarely about what you've already done. It's about what hasn't happened yet: the task you're putting off, the conversation you keep delaying, the responsibility that isn't here yet but your body is already tensing up for.

The nervous system doesn't wait for problems to arrive before responding. It scans ahead and starts preparing.

Cathier's body scan includes a shoulder section with two specific sensation words:

**负重 (fù zhòng)**: the weight of duty settling into muscle — not fatigue from effort, but obligation finding a physical address before the work even begins.

**收紧 (shōu jǐn)**: pre-tension — the "I'm not ready but I have to be" tightening that shows up before the event does.

They feel similar. They're asking very different questions.

Next time your shoulders feel heavy, try one specific question: is this today's weight, or tomorrow's?

That distinction often changes what you do next.
