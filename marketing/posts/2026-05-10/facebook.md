# The difference between joy and happiness — and why it matters.

Most of us use "joy" and "happiness" as if they mean the same thing. But they don't — and once you feel the difference, you can't unfeel it.

Happiness is a reaction. Good news arrives; you feel happy. The news fades; the happiness fades with it. It's entirely tied to what's happening around you.

Joy is different. Joy doesn't depend on events. It's the quiet feeling of fullness when you're sitting with a cup of tea and nothing special is happening — but something inside you feels settled and whole.

In Chinese, there are specific words for different kinds of joy that English simply doesn't have. The Cathier awareness dictionary includes 10 entries in its joy category. Three of them:

雀跃 — A light, upward feeling. The sensation in your body before your mind even registers something good.

沛然 — A sense of inner abundance. Like being filled with something gently in motion.

畅然 — Unobstructed. Clear and flowing, with no tension anywhere.

You've probably felt all three at some point in your life. You just didn't have words for them yet.

That's what naming emotions at this level of precision can do — it helps you notice what's actually there, including the quiet good.
