The emotion isn't the problem. Resisting it is.

Osho said this. It took me a long time to understand what it meant in practice.

Most emotion trackers ask: how are you feeling today? That question invites you to label the summary and skip the signal underneath.

Cathier does something different. It starts with the body. Before you name the emotion, you locate where it lives — the chest that's been tight since morning, the throat that won't quite open, the jaw that hasn't unclenched. Then you choose from 79 specific emotion words. Not "sad," but "wistful," "bereft," or "forlorn" — each a different shape, pointing to a different next step.

Osho's point: when you resist an emotion, you give it more energy. When you witness it precisely — where it is, what it feels like, what it's actually called — it starts to move.

The body scan and emotion naming aren't for fixing anything. They're for seeing clearly.

That's the whole first step.
