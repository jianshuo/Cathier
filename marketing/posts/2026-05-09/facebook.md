# The body stores what we don't process.

Tight shoulders and a low stomach ache might not be physical problems — they might be emotional ones that never found a way out.

Bessel van der Kolk spent decades studying how the body holds what the mind moves past. One of his core observations: emotions we don't express don't disappear. They find somewhere else to live — in our muscles, in our gut, in the tension we carry without knowing why.

That tight jaw at night. The weight in your chest on a Sunday evening. The shoulders that won't quite release no matter how many times you stretch them. Sometimes these are the body's way of holding onto something the mind has already moved on from.

Cathier has an awareness dictionary with 24 body parts. The idea is simple: when something feels off, start with the location rather than the emotion. Because often, the body knows before the mind does.

If you've ever noticed tension that seemed connected to something emotional — even something you thought you'd already dealt with — you're not imagining it. Your body is just keeping its own records.
