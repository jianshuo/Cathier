# Emotion doesn't disappear — it moves in.

Your shoulder tightness? Might not be a muscle problem. Van der Kolk spent decades on this: emotions we don't process don't vanish — they relocate.

The anger you swallowed in that meeting. The grief you didn't let yourself feel in full. The words you chose not to say. They find a new address: the tension across your shoulders, the low ache in your stomach, the jaw that clenches at night.

Cathier has 24 body-part entries in its awareness dictionary. Not anatomy — more like a forwarding directory.

When your jaw is tight, it might be 忿然 (fèn rán) — not anger exactly, but the specific feeling of being misunderstood with no clean way to correct it.
When your stomach drops, it might be 沉郁 (chén yù) — not sadness, but something falling without knowing where from.
When your throat constricts, it might be 酸楚 (suān chǔ) — the moment just before tears that didn't come.

Your body isn't misfiring. It's keeping records.

Next time something aches without explanation — start with the location. Find the word. Hear what it's been holding.
